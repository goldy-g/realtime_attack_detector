  connections, at 2.1–3.7 s per verdict and at most 704 prompt tokens. The probes show its verdict follows the
  session's Remote Desktop origin: remove the session lines and it says normal; rename the account and it still
  says attack. Worked examples add nothing (confidence 95 → 80), so the zero-shot prompt is the one to use.
- Only behind a router. Asked about every benign case (a stress test the router never produces), the specialist
  flags 33 of 86. Folding the examples into the submitted prompt instead (C) misses w01 and the updater attack
  and flags 9 of 86 benign cases, against 2 of 86 and 24 of 25 attacks at their first case for the submitted prompt.
- E2B is not reliable here: it catches only w01 and calls one scripted connection an attack.
- Limits: 3 positives on 2 machines and 14 negatives, all scripted, from local sessions; no benign Remote Desktop
  chain (a jump host) exists in the data, and the specialist would call one an attack. The prompt states the
  chain criterion the RDP-chain expert uses, but the SLM makes the call: every outbound Remote Desktop case
  goes to it, and it separates them by what the case says.
- Coverage gap found on the way: triage makes a case only of a pair's first occurrence in a wave, so an
  attacker's hop on a machine whose scripts already ran `mstsc.exe → 3389` that hour (host-339f) never reaches
  the SLM or the expert.
